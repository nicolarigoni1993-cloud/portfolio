# Ninth Slate — Pricing Framework

Internal only. No public prices yet.

## Current principle

Nicola's marketplace rate may remain lower while building verified platform history.
Ninth Slate should not anchor future studio work to a permanently low hourly rate.

## Pricing models

### 1. Hourly
Good for:
- maintenance
- uncertain debugging
- ongoing collaboration

### 2. Fixed-scope
Preferred for:
- bug fixes with clear reproduction
- small features
- integrations
- QA/release checks

### 3. Milestone-based
Good for:
- internal tools
- larger features
- prototypes

## Quote structure

Every quote should separate:
- scope
- assumptions
- deliverables
- out-of-scope
- timeline
- revision/fix boundary
- deployment inclusion/exclusion

## Early-stage rule

Prioritize:
- achievable jobs
- clear outcomes
- fast feedback
- strong reviews

Avoid:
- underpricing large unknown codebases
- fixed price before inspecting unclear scope
- "unlimited revisions"
- free discovery for complex projects

## Marketplace vs direct

Marketplace:
Accept strategic lower entry pricing when it helps gain verified work history.

Direct/studio:
Price from scope, complexity, risk and value rather than copying marketplace entry rates.

## Future rate review triggers

Increase pricing after any of:
- 2–3 strong verified reviews
- repeat client
- clear niche demand
- measurable delivery speed
- specialized security/AI work
- larger responsibility or production risk
